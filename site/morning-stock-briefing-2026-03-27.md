**Meta Platforms (META) — -7.96%:** Meta suffered its worst single-day drop in over a year after a California jury delivered a landmark ruling finding Meta and Alphabet negligent for designing addictive products that harmed children and teenagers — drawing immediate comparisons to the tobacco industry's liability watershed moment of the 1990s. The verdict sets a precedent that could open the floodgates for thousands of similar lawsuits, with legal experts estimating potential industry-wide liability in the tens of billions. Despite the verdict, Meta's fundamentals remain strong: revenue reached $200.97B (+22.2%) in 2025 and 42 analysts maintain Strong Buy with a $838.50 consensus target (+53% upside from current levels). The gap between the analyst target and current price ($547.54) reflects the market's belief that this legal liability is temporal and manageable — and that Meta's AI monetization thesis (Llama, Meta AI, AR/VR) remains firmly intact over the medium term.

**Micron Technology (MU) — -6.97%:** Micron was the standout loser in the semiconductor space, falling 6.97% Thursday and extending a brutal ~20% five-day drawdown that began when Google published TurboQuant research. The fear: if LLMs require 6x less memory to run, memory chip demand could crater. But this narrative deserves scrutiny — Micron's Q2 2026 results showed revenue up 196% and EPS up 682% year-over-year, powered by HBM3E demand that is capacity-constrained today. Memory compression cuts inference cost for existing models but it doesn't eliminate the need for ever-larger training runs and expanding AI deployment scale. At P/E 16.72 and forward P/E of just 3.93, Micron is priced for a demand collapse that fundamentals do not currently support. The 32-analyst consensus maintains Strong Buy with a $443.47 target (+24.8% upside), and a mean reversion trade looks compelling for patient investors.

### Notable Korea Movers

**The Intraday Semiconductor Reversal** was the defining story of Friday's Korean session. Samsung Electronics fell as much as 4.22% and SK Hynix dropped 5.47% at the open as TurboQuant fears combined with Trump's overnight Iran statement to trigger broad selling across semiconductor names — dragging the KOSPI to an intraday low near 5,200. By mid-session, however, a crucial reversal took hold as savvy institutional buyers absorbed the panic-selling, recognizing that TurboQuant reduces inference memory consumption rather than physical chip demand, and that AI model complexity is growing faster than any compression algorithm can offset. Both stocks recovered to nearly flat by close, delivering a 240–300 point intraday recovery in the KOSPI — a powerful signal that the long-term AI memory thesis remains intact for Korea's semiconductor champions.
